# DBQ: Women's Power in Afro-Eurasia (c. 1200–1450)

## Come to class with:
- **Extent:** Moderate. Women had real power, but it was limited and conditional.
- **Claim 1 (strongest):** Elite women gained political and military power through family position or crisis. Docs 1, 6, 4
- **Claim 2:** Women held economic and social influence where local traditions stayed strong. Docs 5, 2
- **Claim 3 (weakest):** Patriarchal ideas limited women's power. Docs 3, 7

---

## Intro

*(Contextualization)* From 1200 to 1450, belief systems like Islam, Neo-Confucianism, and Christianity spread across Afro-Eurasia and generally reinforced patriarchy. At the same time, the Mongol conquests and growing trade networks connected societies with very different traditions, some of which gave women more freedom. This meant women's roles varied a lot depending on the region.

*(Thesis)* Women exercised power to a moderate extent during this period. Elite women gained political and military authority through family ties or crisis, and women in some regions held economic and social influence because of local traditions. However, patriarchal belief systems still limited women's power and often made it temporary.

---

## Body Paragraph 1

*(Claim)* Elite women gained political and military power through their families or during times of crisis.

*(Describe Doc 1)* Juvaini writes that after her husband died, Sorqotani Beki controlled the Mongol army and state affairs and paid for a madrasa in Bukhara.
*(HIPP – Point of View)* Juvaini was a Persian official working for the Mongols, under Sorqotani's son Hulegu, so he had a reason to praise her family. Even so, he shows that the Khan himself gave her this authority.
*(Support)* This shows that a woman could hold real political power through her royal family position.

*(Describe Doc 6)* Joan of Arc, a peasant girl, writes to the King of England as "commander of the military" and threatens to drive his troops out of France.
*(HIPP – Purpose)* Her purpose was to scare the English into leaving. She claims she was sent by God because, as a peasant woman, she had no other way to justify commanding an army.
*(Support)* This shows a woman could gain military power during a crisis like the Hundred Years' War, but she needed extraordinary justification to do it.

*(Describe Doc 4)* The drawing shows Tomoe Gozen, a female samurai, armored on horseback during the Genpei War.
*(HIPP – Historical Situation)* It was drawn in the 1700s, about 500 years after she lived, when Japan romanticized its warrior past. So it shows more about how she was remembered than what women usually did.
*(Support)* It still shows that a woman warrior was celebrated during a time of war.

*(Outside Evidence)* Similarly, Shajar al-Durr ruled Egypt as sultana in 1250 after her husband died. This is another case of a woman gaining power through family and crisis.

## Body Paragraph 2
- **Claim:** Women held economic and social influence where local traditions stayed strong.
- **Doc 5:** Zhou Daguan says that in Cambodia, all the traders were women.
  - **HIPP (Audience):** He was writing for a Chinese audience, where women rarely traded, so this stood out to him.
  - **Support:** It shows that women had economic power in the Khmer Empire.
- **Doc 2:** Ibn Battuta says that Malian women didn't veil, had male friends, and that inheritance went through the mother's side.
  - **HIPP (Point of View):** He was a Muslim scholar who judged them by strict Islamic norms and called their customs "outlandish." His disapproval proves how much freedom they actually had.
  - **Support:** It shows that local West African traditions gave women social independence.

---

## Body Paragraph 3
- **Claim:** Patriarchal belief systems limited women's power.
- **Doc 3:** Empress Xu teaches that women should be modest and obey their husbands.
  - **HIPP (Purpose):** She wrote to teach palace women and to restore Confucian order after Mongol rule.
  - **Support:** It shows that Neo-Confucianism expected women to submit, even though she herself had influence by writing it.
- **Doc 7:** Juzjani says Raziyya had every quality of a great king, but being a woman made it pointless.
  - **HIPP (Point of View):** He was a male court historian reflecting the elite men who overthrew her in 1240.
  - **Support:** It shows that even a capable female ruler lost power because of her gender.

---

## Conclusion (optional)

Between 1200 and 1450, women's power was real but limited. Elite women and women in places with strong local traditions had influence, but patriarchal beliefs kept most women's power rare and short-lived.
